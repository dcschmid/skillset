# Creative writing & dialogue (separate law)

Never optimize a story by README criteria. Rules here serve believability,
not cleanliness.

## Avoid

Characters narrating their own feelings instead of showing them; everyone
speaking in the same register (the author's); conversations where every
question gets its answer; everyday scenes dressed in unnecessary poetry;
emotional summaries after each beat; cliché conflict; resolved-too-tidy
endings; constant tempo; metaphor confetti; closing lines that pose as
profound; restating motivation the reader already has.

## Prefer

Distinct character voices (word load, sentence length, interruptions differ
per speaker); subtext — what is NOT said carries the scene; reactions that
arrive late or wrong; interruptions, restarts, half-finished sentences;
emotion sized to the event; scene cuts without explanation; conflicts that
end open when life would; precise observed detail over general mood;
character change that costs something; silence and pause as pacing; POV held
consistently unless the shift is the technique.

## Protection

Deliberate devices (leitmotifs, running gags, structured repetition that
payoff later, genre rhythm — short punchy prose for thrillers) are content,
not slop. The linter's rhythm rules do not apply inside fiction unless the
user asks for a pass; when they do, apply density checks paragraph-wise,
never line-wise, and report — not rewrite — the device passages.

## Voice diversity test

Cover each speaker's name: can you still tell who talks? If not, scene not
done. Then check: is any paragraph a summary of the one before it? Cut the
later one.
