# English rule set (native, not translated)

Standard: US or UK per project config; keep one per document ("organize"
vs "organise" never mixed).

## Detection list (hints, not bans)

in today's fast-paced world · it's important to note · let's dive/delve
into · at its core · here's the thing · the ever-evolving landscape · a
testament to · game-changer · unlock the potential · seamlessly integrates ·
it's not just X, it's Y · navigate the complexities · a vibrant tapestry ·
what nobody tells you · let that sink in · in conclusion · this highlights
the importance of · a powerful reminder · the future isn't coming · whether
you're a · plays a crucial role · needless to say.

Scoring matches `patterns-core.md`: isolated hit = informational, cluster =
edit. Plain "is/are/has" beats "serves as/boasts". "use" beats "utilize".

## Structural tells

- One-line dramatic fragments as habit ("That's it. That's the whole thing.")
- Colon reveals ("The best part: it learns.") once per document max.
- LinkedIn-close ("I hope this helps. What has your experience been?").
- Stacked h2/h3 every 60 words; bullets where two sentences belong.
- Em/en dash several per page — allowed in prose, flagged as rhythm signal.

## Register

Technical docs: verbs first, units exact, commands in backticks, no booster
adjectives before results. Editorial: voice stays; cut only echoes.
Marketing: the specific number replaces the adjective ("3.5 MB/s on a
laptop", never "blazing fast").

## Quality questions per paragraph

1. Is there a checkable claim here (number, name, mechanism, source)?
2. Would a careful writer say it shorter to this reader?
3. Does this paragraph repeat the previous one in other words?
